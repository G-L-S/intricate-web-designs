FIELD NOTES — MIDWEST CORRIDOR (Q3 2024)
==========================================

County 1: Harlan County, NE
County 2: Pawnee County, KS
County 3: Harrison County, IA

Three counties. Three states. Same pattern.

Within 18 months of new "seed certification" requirements being adopted at the
state level, every independent seed supplier in these counties either lost their
contracts or closed entirely. The timing is not coincidental.

Key observations:

- All three counties adopted near-identical certification language within a
  six-month window (Jan–June 2023). The draft language traces back to model
  legislation circulated by a single industry group.

- Independent suppliers were given 90 days to comply with testing requirements
  that cost between $14,000 and $22,000 per product line. The majors had
  pre-certified inventories ready before the legislation was even introduced.

- Two of three county extension offices confirmed off-record that the
  certification standards were "ichosen to match existing product lines" of the
  dominant supplier. No one would say this on the record.

- In Harrison County, the last independent supplier — Bridger Seeds, family
  operation since 1971 — shut down in August 2024. The owner told me: "They
  didn't beat us. They just made sure we couldn't play."

Pattern: Regulatory capture at the county level, using state-level certification
as the mechanism. The cost of compliance is the weapon.

This is not the first time. See: poultry integration (1990s), hog consolidation
(2000s), dairy cooperative collapse (2010s). The playbook is the same. Only the
crop changes.

— G.L.S.
